How Anaheim Homeowners Can Extend Water Heater Life

The best way to avoid a surprise leak is simple maintenance. We recommend that homeowners flush the tank once a year (more often with hard water), have the anode rod checked every three to five years, lift the T&P valve lever once a year to make sure it isn't stuck, and keep the thermostat around 120 degrees, which limits scalding risk and cuts down on scale. Tankless owners should plan on a descaling flush once a year if their water is hard.

Take a look at the age of your water heater. The rating plate on the tank usually includes the month and year it was built, and its age tells you a lot. Most tank heaters make it to 8 to 12 years, heat pump models 10 to 15, and well-maintained tankless units about 20. If your heater is beyond that point and also shows rusty water, rumbling, or moisture at the base, it's a strong candidate for a planned replacement, and that's almost always cheaper and easier on everyone than a rushed emergency install once the tank fails.

If warning signs do show up, acting quickly keeps them small. A dripping relief valve might point to high water pressure or thermal expansion with nowhere to go, both inexpensive fixes. The same drip left alone for a season can keep stressing the tank until a seam gives way, and a small repair becomes a full replacement plus water damage. It's smart to keep our number saved on your phone so you can reach us at the first sign of trouble. Early intervention saves time, money, and cold showers.

Typical Water Heater Issues in Anaheim

Most water heater failures in Anaheim give warning signs before they happen. Pay attention to a popping or rumbling sound while the tank heats, rust-colored hot water, a faint rotten-egg smell, moisture around the base, or hot water that runs out a little sooner each month. These are early signs of heavy sediment, a depleted anode, a weak element or burner, and early corrosion, and caught early, most of them mean a modest repair or a replacement on your schedule. Left alone, they end in a ruptured tank and a cleanup bill. Pay close attention to a relief valve that drips or discharges: it means the tank is seeing too much pressure or temperature, and it needs a professional look soon. We diagnose the cause, not just the symptom, so small problems stay small.
