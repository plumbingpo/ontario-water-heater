No hot water from a gas heater usually means one thing: the burner is not firing, and something told it not to. Gas heaters are built around safety. The control valve will not open unless a thermocouple or flame sensor proves there is a flame, and many models lock out if the flame arrestor screen at the bottom gets clogged or the heater senses spilled exhaust. So the real question is which safety did its job, and why. Across Ontario, CA, the answer is most often a worn thermocouple, a dirty pilot or burner, a lint-choked air intake, a failing gas valve, or a vent that is no longer drafting properly.

Ontario Water Heater does not guess with gas appliances. On every Ontario call, our technicians test the thermocouple's millivolt output, inspect the pilot and burner flame, check gas pressure at the valve, and run a draft test at the draft hood to confirm exhaust is actually leaving the house. We look inside the combustion chamber for scorching, rust, and signs of flame rollout, which tell us whether the flue is blocked or the burner is misbehaving, and we check for carbon monoxide around the heater. One rule comes before all of it: if you smell gas, do not light anything or flip switches. Leave the house, call your gas utility from outside, and then call us once it is safe.

Once we know the cause, the repair is usually simple. A new thermocouple or flame sensor, a cleaned pilot tube and burner, a cleared flame arrestor screen, or a replacement gas control valve gets most heaters running again the same day. Vent problems, like a disconnected connector or a flue pipe without enough rise, are corrected on the spot. If sediment is rumbling in the tank, we flush it, and if the anode rod is spent, we can replace it. When replacement is the smarter move, we install new gas heaters sized to your household's first-hour demand, with proper atmospheric, power vent, or direct vent venting, a drip leg on the gas supply, and the expansion tank, drain pan, and strapping your local code calls for. Signs You Need Gas Water Heater Services in Ontario

Recognizing the warning signs early can save you money and keep a slow drip from turning into a flooded utility room. These are the red flags our technicians see most often before a breakdown:

  • Less hot water than the tank should deliver
  • Pilot outages that keep coming back after a relight
  • A silent heater with no flame and no hot water
  • Exhaust odor, rust, or moisture around the draft hood and vent pipe
  • Popping, rumbling, or crackling sounds as the burner heats
  • Leaks at the drain valve, the fittings, or the bottom of the tank
  • Any smell of gas around the appliance, which calls for leaving and phoning the gas utility
  • A yellow, lazy, or sooty burner flame instead of a crisp blue one

Why This Job Calls for a Pro

Start with the hazards. Every gas water heater involves fuel piping, an open flame, and exhaust that contains carbon monoxide. A loose flare or threaded joint can leak gas, a vent that is disconnected or poorly sloped can spill exhaust back into the room, and a gas valve installed without a leak test is a risk you cannot see. Then there is the diagnostic side: a pilot that goes out can mean a weak thermocouple, a dirty pilot orifice, a clogged flame arrestor, a draft problem, or a failing gas valve, and working through them by guesswork is slow and costly.

Our technicians approach gas heaters with instruments rather than assumptions. They check thermocouple output in millivolts, read gas pressure at the valve, test draft at the hood, check for CO around the appliance, and leak-test every joint they touch. The result is a repair that targets the real fault and a home that is safer for it. We also give you an honest assessment of the heater itself. A tank past about ten or twelve years with a failing valve or a leak is usually a replacement, and you will hear that from us before you spend money on a repair.

Simple Ways to Extend Your Water Heater's Life

A few preventive steps can help you avoid a cold shower or a wet floor down the road. Here are our recommendations:

  • Keep working CO alarms near bedrooms and on each floor
  • Flush the tank on a regular schedule so sediment never bakes onto the bottom
  • Make sure the vent pipe stays connected, sloped upward, and free of rust
  • Choose a setting of about 120 degrees to balance comfort, safety, and efficiency
  • Keep the area around the heater clear, especially of gasoline, paint, and solvents
  • Check the anode rod every three to five years, since it protects the tank from corrosion
  • Keep the air intake at the bottom of the heater free of lint and dust
