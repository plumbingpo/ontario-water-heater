Going tankless changes how your home makes hot water. There is no tank to keep hot, so there is no standby loss while you sleep or travel. When a faucet or shower opens, the unit senses flow and fires, heating water as it moves through a heat exchanger. The result is a continuous supply for as long as you stay within the unit's rating, a compact footprint on the wall, and a lifespan that can be nearly double that of a typical tank. Families across Ontario, CA often choose tankless during a replacement or remodel for exactly those reasons.

Undersizing is the number one tankless mistake we see. A unit rated for plenty of flow on a spring day can struggle in winter, when incoming water is much colder and the heater has to raise it many more degrees. So we size for your coldest months and your busiest moments, counting the fixtures that run together. We also confirm the gas line and meter can feed a burner that may be rated near 199,000 BTU, choose the correct venting for the specific unit, and plan a condensate drain with a neutralizer where needed. For electric tankless, we check the panel first, because whole-home models can outstrip an older electrical service.

Every tankless install from Ontario Water Heater includes isolation service valves so future descaling takes minutes instead of hours, proper gas piping with a drip leg, correctly pitched and terminated venting, and a pressure relief valve piped to a safe location. If your water is hard, we talk through scale protection, since mineral buildup is what shortens tankless life most. We can add recirculation for faster hot water at distant bathrooms, set the temperature, handle permits where required, and walk you through maintenance before we leave. Symptoms That Mean It's Time to Call

Most tanks announce trouble weeks or months ahead with rumbling noises, rusty water, or hot showers that keep getting shorter. Keep an eye out for the following:

  • A distant bath or detached space that needs its own hot water source
  • Standby losses on a tank that sits idle for long stretches
  • Adding a bathroom, soaking tub, or larger shower that needs more hot water
  • A water heater near the end of its service life, making this a natural time to switch
  • Showers turning lukewarm before everyone gets a turn
  • Limited floor space in a closet, garage, or utility room
  • Wanting a unit that can last up to twice as long as a typical tank
  • A home remodel or new build where the gas line and venting can be planned from scratch

What a Trained Technician Brings to the Job

Buying the right unit is the easy part. The other half is making sure your home can support it. Gas tankless burners can fire at close to 199,000 BTU, so the size of the gas line, the capacity of the meter, and the pressure at high fire all have to check out. Vent materials, lengths, and terminations are set by the manual and the code, and condensing units add a condensate drain that should be neutralized. Electric whole-home units can pull more current than an older panel can deliver. Miss one of these, and the unit underperforms or becomes unsafe.

Our job is to make tankless work the way it is supposed to. That starts with sizing for your busiest hour and coldest water, then confirming your gas, venting, and electrical can support the unit. We install with service valves for descaling, proper relief valve discharge, and venting built to the manual, then test under real demand before we leave. If a tankless unit is not the best choice for your home, whether because of cost, gas supply, or usage patterns, we say so and help you compare other options honestly.

Prevention Tips That Actually Help

Routine maintenance is almost always cheaper and easier than an early replacement. Here's where to begin:

  • Protect outdoor or garage units from freezing, and keep power on during cold snaps so freeze protection works
  • If the unit shows a recurring code, note it and have it checked
  • Set the temperature around 120F for safety and efficiency
  • Check the vent cap outside for nests, debris, or buildup
  • Descale the heat exchanger once a year, or more often with hard water
  • Check and rinse the small filter screen at the cold inlet periodically
  • Consider a water softener or scale-prevention filter if your water is hard
