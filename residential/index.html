Clear Pricing

Straight Answers and Upfront Prices

Good water heater service starts with knowing exactly what you're dealing with. Ontario Water Heater provides honest, upfront pricing on every job, handling everything from a failed heating element to a full tank replacement. Every visit begins with a thorough assessment of your unit, including its age, capacity, fuel type, venting, and valve and tank condition, followed by a clear recommendation and a written price before any work starts.

Many water heater problems stay hidden until a failure is well underway. Sediment builds up on the bottom of the tank, the anode rod quietly corrodes away, and the steel underneath starts to rust from the inside with no visible warning. If you notice rusty hot water, popping or rumbling sounds, a rotten-egg smell, shorter hot showers than you're used to, or moisture around the base, schedule an inspection sooner rather than later. Catching it early often keeps a repairable issue from turning into a flooded floor.

Do you know how old your water heater is? The serial number on the label usually tells us. When a tank gets past the 10-year mark, it's worth planning a replacement before the current one decides for you. For reference, a 40 to 50 gallon gas tank typically runs $1,400–$2,800 installed, and an electric tank typically runs $1,100–$2,400, depending on venting, code updates, and access. Our team will tell you plainly whether yours has years left or is running on borrowed time.

Preventive Care

Tank Flushing and Tune-Ups That Pay Off

Routine maintenance is the most reliable way to keep hot water flowing year-round. Regular service prevents failures before they start, flushes out mineral sediment, and catches small faults while they're still inexpensive to fix. At Ontario Water Heater, every maintenance visit is built around your actual heater, covering a full tank flush, an anode rod inspection, a test of the temperature and pressure relief valve, a burner and flue check on gas units, element and thermostat testing on electric units, and a thermostat setting check.

Many homes do best with one flush a year, and homes with hard water may need a visit every six months. The schedule flexes with the age of your equipment, how hard your household runs it, and local water quality, which determines how fast sediment accumulates and how long an anode rod lasts.

Homeowners are often surprised how much a regular maintenance schedule helps. A clean tank heats water faster with less noise, a healthy anode rod protects the steel from corrosion, and a working relief valve protects the whole house. Most families find that a maintenance plan costs far less than replacing a water heater even a few years early.

Sized for Your Household

Hot Water Sized to Your Family's Demand

Bigger isn't always better when it comes to water heaters. What matters for a tank is the first-hour rating, how many gallons of hot water it can deliver in one busy hour, while for a tankless unit it's the GPM it can sustain at your incoming water temperature. We don't simply copy the gallon rating of the old heater. Instead, we look at how many people live in the home, how many bathrooms you have, and big draws like a soaking tub or a high-capacity washer.

Choosing a new water heater is about more than the number of gallons. Our technician will compare tankless units against a traditional tank, and gas against electric or hybrid, walking through operating costs, venting and gas line work, electrical capacity, and the installation itself. Tankless is a strong fit for many families, but it often needs a larger gas line and new venting, while a properly sized tank is often the simpler, lower-cost choice. The decision is yours, based on your home and budget.

Repairs We Handle

The Water Heater Repairs Local Homes Need Most

A cold tap where hot water should be, a burner that refuses to light, a rumbling, sediment-heavy tank, a slow drip from the relief valve: every one of these problems has a different cause and needs its own repair. Ontario Water Heater diagnoses the exact fault before replacing anything, whether it's an electric tank with a burned-out element, a thermocouple that no longer holds the pilot, a broken dip tube mixing cold water into the hot, or a tankless unit throwing error codes.

Some problems are just a nuisance, while others pose real risks. A burned-out element simply leaves you with lukewarm water, but a relief valve that keeps discharging is warning you that temperature or pressure inside the tank is too high, and a thermostat set above 120°F can scald a child in seconds. If you ever smell gas near the heater, leave the house and call your gas utility from outside before you call us. We match the fix to the failure: elements and thermostats on electric tanks, thermocouples, flame sensors, and gas valves on gas units, expansion tanks where water pressure is spiking, and leak repair on drain valves, fittings, and relief lines.
